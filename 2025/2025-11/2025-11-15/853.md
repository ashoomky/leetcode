another monotonic stack question - car fleet!

two input arrays:
position - the position[i] is the starting mile of the ith car
speed - the speed[i] is the speed of the ith car in miles per hour
target - the number of miles each n cars are trying to reach (n = len(position))

a car cannot pass another car's speed but it can go at the speed of the next slowest car

a car fleet is a single car or a group of cars driving next to each other, where the speed of the car fleet is the minimum speed of any car in the fleet

return the number of car fleets (groups) that will arrive at the destination

okay but how do you find out when they meet and become a fleet?
ohhh because when you add together the position and speed of the car you get a number of miles where they meet each other. 
e.g. car start at 10 (speed 2) and 8 (speed 4) become a fleet, meeting each other at 12.
e.g. car starting at 5 (speed 1) and 3 (speed 3) becomes a fleet, meeting each other at 6. fleet moves at speed 1 until it reaches target.

hint1 says to sort the positions of the car in decreasing order. 
hint 2 says we do this because a car can only form a fleet with another car ahead of it, which sorting in this order gives clarity about the final speed of each car. 
hint 3 says to use the formula: time = (target - position) / speed. now use this to identify that two cars will form a fleet, as it can only do that if and only if the car ahead has a time that is greater than or equal to the time of the car behind it. how to keep track of the total number of fleets happened while going through the array?
hint 4 says to use a stack to maintain the times of the fleets. iterate through the array (sorted in descending order of positions), compute the time for each car to reach the target and check if it can form a fleet with the car ahead. if current car's time is less than or equal to the top of the stack, it joins the fleet. otherwise it forms a new fleet, and push its time onto the stack. length of the stack will then represent the total number of fleets formed.

i tried putting the position and speed into list pairs within a new array called cars, then sort it by the first index which is positions.

i did that but then got stuck on the monotonic decreasing stack logic. i looked at the answers and i got the first half right, but the second half i got the logic wrong. basically you append the pair first, then check that there are more than two elements in the stack to compare AND that if the last element is less than or equal to the second to last element (as no car behind the current one can surpass it), then pop because it must collide and therefore be part of the same fleet.

i also realised i needed to convert the speed to a float so that the value would come out as a decimal, and the comparison would be more accurate. 

we iterate through the input list in reverse by position as when a collision is detected, we assume the car continues to move at the same speed whereas it doesn't and only moves at the speed of the slowest car.

```python
class Solution(object):
    def carFleet(self, target, position, speed):
        """
        :type target: int
        :type position: List[int]
        :type speed: List[int]
        :rtype: int
        """

        cars = [(p, s) for p, s in zip(position, speed)]  # [position, speed]
        cars.sort(reverse = True)

        time_stack = []
        for pos, speed in cars:
            time = (target - pos) / float(speed)
            time_stack.append(time)
            if len(time_stack) >= 2 and time_stack[-1] <= time_stack[-2]:
                # means they collide, so pop off the stack because it's technically part of one fleet now
                time_stack.pop()

        return len(time_stack)
```
